Work through the reasoning

x[n] = cos(2π · f0 · n / Fs + φ)

Step 1 — Compute the folding frequency

Compute the remainder of f0 modulo Fs and fold it into the interval [0, Fs/2]. Frequencies differing by an integer number of sampling rates are indistinguishable in the sampled complex exponential. Real cosine frequencies may also reflect about the Nyquist frequency, with phase/sign consequences.

Step 2 — Test an explicit counterexample

At 40 samples/sec, a zero-phase 12 Hz cosine and 28 Hz cosine yield identical sequences: cos(2π·12n/40) = cos(2π·28n/40) at every integer n. Change the phase to see why the simple reflected positive-frequency cosine can differ in sign of phase.

Step 3 — What about the Nyquist boundary?

At exactly f0 = Fs/2, the sampled cosine becomes (−1)n cos φ; its quadrature cannot be recovered from these real samples. The usual strict bandlimit/sampling condition requires Fs > 2 fmax, together with the relevant signal assumptions.
