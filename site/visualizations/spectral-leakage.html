Understand each transformation

1. Observe and taper a finite sequence

x[n] = cos(2πfn + φ), n = 0, …, N−1; y[n] = w[n]x[n].

Rectangular weights are 1. The symmetric Hann weights are 0.5(1 − cos(2πn/(N−1))). Multiplying samples by the window corresponds to periodic convolution in frequency.

2. Evaluate the discrete Fourier transform

Y[k] = ∑ y[n] e−j2πkn/N; fk = k/N.

The graph shows one-sided amplitudes |Y[k]|/sum(w), doubled for interior positive-frequency bins. DC and Nyquist bins are not doubled. This coherent-gain correction is illustrative, not a power spectral density normalization.

3. Check the energy identity

∑ |w[n]x[n]|² = (1/N) ∑k=0N−1 |Y[k]|².

Parseval's identity holds for either window, including when the input frequency is off-grid. The difference reported above should be near numerical floating-point precision.

4. Examine exceptions and tradeoffs

At a non-DC/Nyquist integer-bin cosine, a rectangular window aligns the signal with DFT bins. Shift it by half a bin: the sidelobes spread across frequencies. A Hann window suppresses far sidelobes but broadens the main lobe and changes apparent peak height; it does not magically eliminate all leakage, fix stochastic variance, or create new frequency resolution. Zero-padding could interpolate the display without adding information.
