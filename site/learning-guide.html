Original mastery checklist

Use these as self-assessment prompts rather than assignment solutions. Explain the assumptions and test at least one failure case before relying on a result.

  • Sampling and dimensions: Can you convert between physical frequency in Hz and normalized frequency in cycles/sample? Can you identify what aliases after decimation?
  • Stochastic processes: Can you state what wide-sense stationarity implies for mean, autocorrelation and power spectral density? How does a finite realization differ from an ensemble?
  • Linear estimation: Can you demonstrate the error-orthogonality condition for a linear-MMSE estimate, and explain what changes when its covariance matrix is singular?
  • Prediction: Can you distinguish forward and backward sample indexing and verify a low-order Toeplitz solve against an independently calculated covariance system?
  • Finite-record spectra: Can you derive the periodogram's frequency normalization and explain spectral leakage without treating a single estimate as the true PSD?
  • Bias/variance: Can you distinguish increasing record length from averaging independent estimates? How do window length and number of segments trade frequency resolution against variance?
  • Model choice: Can you identify the stationarity and AR-order assumptions behind parametric spectral estimation and explain what model mismatch does to the estimate?
  • Evidence quality: Can you state which conclusion is mathematical, which is synthetic simulation evidence and which depends on the observed course example?
Normalized frequency convention: f = ω / (2π) cycles/sample; physical frequency F = f × Fs Hz for sampling rate Fs samples/second.

The exact November 16 class discussion explicitly uses normalized non-angular frequency f = ω/(2π). Some other conventions in external references may differ.

One original reasoning exercise

Scenario: You record independent, zero-mean, real Gaussian white-noise samples with variance σ² and estimate spectral power at zero frequency.

Why doesn't one longer periodogram automatically become reliable?

For the specific model, the normalized zero-frequency sum is standard Gaussian, so its squared magnitude has a chi-square distribution with one degree of freedom. Its variance remains 2σ⁴ regardless of sample length; increasing the record length does not make this single zero-frequency estimate consistent.

What changes when independent blocks are averaged?

With K independent blocks, the average retains mean σ² and its variance becomes 2σ⁴/K. That exact reduction relies on the independence and Gaussian assumptions; overlapping or correlated segments require separate analysis. Increasing K while total data is fixed also shortens each block, affecting frequency resolution for colored spectra.

Experiment with the original periodogram explorer to change K, block length, noise level and seed, then compare empirical with analytic results.
